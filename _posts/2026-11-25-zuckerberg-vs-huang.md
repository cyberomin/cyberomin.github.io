Except you have been off of social media in the last three weeks, you have probably come across the news that rocked the headlines in the last couple of days; Jensen Huang's &mdash; the founder and CEO of Nvidia &mdash; resignation and subsequent reinstatement at Nvidia. 

The one question I have heard from a few friends and family alike is; how could they force him to resign, it's his company. Well, Jensen isn't the first to be fired from the company he co-founded, history is littered with tales of people that were fired by their companies and in most cases, their subsequent return; Steve Jobs of Apple, Sam Altman of OpenAI, Travis Kalanick of Uber, just to name a few.

Last week, I wrote about stock options and what it means to employees, today, I will talk about share types and how they affects founders. In this article, I will attempt to explain why some founders can be fired easily by their board, while for some, the board will have a hard time firing them, e.g, Mark Zuckerberg of Meta.

#### Share structure and voting right.
From a layman's point of view, the number of shares you own in a business will determine the power you wield in the said business. This means, the person with 51% of a business shares will automatically own 51% of the voting right, there by having more power and authority compared to the person with the 49% of the shares and voting right of the business. This 2% difference in shareholding and voting makes all the difference.  

In the case of Nvidia, the company has one principal class of publicly traded shares and this shares carry a 1-to-1 structure. This means, each share carries one voting right. 

In practical terms: If you own 100 Nvidia shares, you have 100 votes. 

Jensen Huang, according to the company's 2026 annual report, owns about 3.58% of Nvidia shares. This translate to **870,604,104** Nvidia common shares. This means, he has 870,604,104 votes. You get the idea now. 

Vanguard and BlackRock, on the hand, own 7.43% and 7.31% respectively, which translates to about 1,806,000,000 shares and 1,777,000,000 shares respectively. Going by the 1-to1 structure of shares to voting rights ratio we mentioned above, Jensen doesn't have that much voting rights.  

Let's look at Meta, another amazing company. Meta has a different share structure consisting of multiple share classes; Class A, Class B and Class C. 

Meta's Class A shares are publicly traded and they carry a 1-to-1 structure. Meaning, each share carries one voting right. It's Class B shares on the hand aren't publicly traded, they are controlled by the founder, Mark Zuckerberg. The shares carry a 1-to10 structure. Meaning, each shares carries ten voting rights, that ten times the voting power of Class A shares. This explains why Zuckerberg has unilateral control and can't be ousted easily. The company's Class C shares carries no voting rights. They are generally issued as part of employee's compensation and they can't dilute the voting power of existing share holders. 

Let's break it down

| Share Class |   Shareholder     | Voting Right |
| ----------- | ---------------   | ------------ |
| Class A     |   Public Investor | 1            |
| Class B     |   Mark Zuckerberg | 10           |
| Class C     |   Everyone else   | 0            |

Let's imagine Meta had 100 shares

| Shareholder     |    Share Type | Shares | Votes |
| --------------- | ------------- | ------ | ----- |
| Public Investor |    Class A    | 80     | 80    |
| Zuckerberg      |    Class B    | 20     | 200   |

Although Zuckerberg owns only 20% of the shares, he controls 71.4% of the votes &mdash; (200 / 280) x 100

#### Conclusion
According to Meta's 2026 SEC filing, Zuckerberg owns:
- 341,215,148 Class B shares.
- 1,220,703 Class A shares.
- **Voting ownership** - approximately 60.6% of Meta's total voting power, based on the filing's stated record date.
- **Economic ownership** - approximately 60.6% of the voting power. 
In other words, his influence over the company is substantially greater than his financial ownership percentage. This is why ordinary investors can't just organise themselves and throw him out, but Jensen, on the hand, can be thrown out.
